What we do

The right transport plan for the patient — not just the aircraft.

Medical suitability, distance, timing, equipment, airport access and receiving-facility logistics all shape the mission.

✈

Fixed-Wing Air Ambulance

Dedicated medical transport by appropriately configured aircraft for domestic and international missions.

◫

Commercial Medical Escort

Physician or paramedic escort on scheduled airlines when the patient's condition and airline requirements allow it.

✚

HEMS & Rotary Wing

Helicopter-based medical transport and operational coordination when geography or time makes rotor-wing response appropriate.

↗

Medical & Logistics Coordination

Medical review, ground transport, equipment, airline paperwork, receiving-facility coordination and mission planning in one workflow.

Life above all. The mission begins with the patient.

Aviation is one part of the solution. The medical plan, transfer environment and end-to-end coordination matter just as much.

Built around the mission

Bedside thinking at aviation speed.

1

Clinical review first

The team reviews the patient's condition, oxygen and equipment needs, mobility, recent procedures and likely in-flight risks.

2

Choose the transport model

Dedicated aircraft, commercial escort, helicopter or another route is selected based on medical and operational fit.

3

Coordinate the whole journey

Origin, destination, ambulance legs, crew, documentation and timing are brought together into one mission plan.

How it works

From request to coordinated transport.

The public flight request feeds directly into Hatzolah Air's mission-intake workflow for review by the operations and medical team.

Submit the request

Provide patient, contact, origin/destination and medical information through the secure intake form.

Medical review

The team reviews the available records and determines what additional information is needed.

Mission planning

Transport mode, route, crew, equipment, ground legs and operational constraints are assessed.

Confirm & coordinate

Once medically and operationally accepted, the mission can move into final coordination and execution.
